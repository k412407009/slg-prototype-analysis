## Result

The two-stage validation ran as separated work:

- Stage 1 created simplified reverse pitch materials and did not score the products.
- Stage 2 scored the locked Stage 1 materials and did not browse for current success, revenue, ranking, downloads, or publisher reputation.

Stage 2 used the Markdown sources as text equivalents because local PDF text extraction was unavailable. The PDFs were generated from those same Markdown files.

## What This Says About The Rubric

The rubric did not simply max-score every known market product. Only Candidate A and Candidate B crossed the 80-point line; Candidate C/D/E stayed in small-sample validation because their Stage 1 materials had specific unresolved gaps.

The current system mainly rewards three things:

1. A front-end hook that is playable and visible in D0-D1.
2. A backend transition where the theme naturally explains alliance, territory, resource, or season pressure.
3. Monetization anchors that come from the player's visible failure reason, not only generic power sales.

Candidate A scored highest because the survival premise connects the furnace, residents, resources, heroes, treatment, alliance help, and territory pressure into one coherent chain.

Candidate B crossed the line because salvage, production, raft expansion, fleet/cargo, and sea-resource conflict form a clear light-management-to-SLG path, but it sits exactly at the threshold because backend attack rules and D0-D30 cadence remain unresolved.

Candidate C scored lowest because the plague-doctor identity is strong, but the backend proof, source consistency, disease-sensitivity risk, and long-term monetization depth are weaker.

Candidate D and E landed in the middle. They have strong entry patterns, but Candidate D is a dual-sample reference rather than one clean pitch, and Candidate E depends heavily on current-version backward inference and still needs differentiation proof.

## Remaining Biases

This was separated, but not fully blind.

- Candidate names were still visible in the Stage 1 PDFs.
- Stage 1 used current public materials when early materials were unavailable.
- Stage 2 used Markdown text equivalents of the PDFs.
- Some source pages contain current product information even when the material avoided using current success as a score input.

For a stricter second run, anonymize product names, remove publisher names, and freeze evidence to archived early-version pages only.

## Calibration Notes

The current decision bands look directionally useful:

- 80+ means the pitch material is coherent enough for 4-8 week validation, not full production.
- 65-79 catches products with a clear hook but unresolved backend, monetization, or evidence gaps.
- The system currently penalizes missing early cadence data, which is correct for pre-launch evaluation.

One watch item: Candidate B at exactly 80 is borderline. If the scoring policy becomes stricter about early-version-only proof, it should probably fall into the high 70s until real D0-D30 cadence and attack-rule evidence are confirmed.
